marko

AR navigation for indoor spaces, built from a floor plan and a single phone scan. Hold up your phone and marko works out where you are from the camera alone, with no beacons and no QR codes, then draws your route right onto the hallway.

01The problem

Indoor maps show you the layout, but not where you are or which way to go. GPS stops working at the front door. The systems that do work indoors rely on Bluetooth beacons or kiosks, which cost venues tens of thousands of dollars to install and maintain, so most malls, hospitals, and campuses simply don't have them.

We wanted indoor navigation that a venue could set up in an afternoon with a phone.

02How it works

  1. ScanOne walk through the building with an iPhone records video, motion data, and LiDAR depth.
  2. Build a digital twinKeyframes are matched and triangulated into a 3D model (structure-from-motion), fused with the LiDAR into a dense point cloud and a walkable graph of the floor.
  3. LocateA photo from the user's camera is matched against the twin. Dense feature matching plus a PnP pose solve gives position and heading, and strict inlier checks reject uncertain fixes.
  4. RouteDijkstra's algorithm finds the shortest path across the walkable graph to any of the labelled places.
  5. GuideAn AR ribbon on the floor, a Find My-style arrow, and a voice assistant that answers "where can I grab lunch?"

03The app

Search for a room, restroom, or café and marko locks on with one camera fix, then keeps tracking with ARKit as you walk. Distance and the next turn stay pinned to the bottom of the screen.

04The business

Setup is free for the venue. marko earns money from what navigation reveals: a dashboard showing foot traffic, what visitors search for and fail to find, and where people get lost. Malls come first, then hospitals and universities.
